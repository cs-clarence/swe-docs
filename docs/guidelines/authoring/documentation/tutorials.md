# Tutorials

## Scope

Documents that teach a newcomer by guiding them through one working outcome. For finishing a task the reader already understands, see [how-to-guides](how-to-guides.md).

## Guidelines

### Lead to one concrete outcome

Pick one result the reader can see working, and build the whole tutorial toward it. A tutorial with several goals teaches none of them well.

### State the starting point

List what the reader needs and the state they begin in: tools, versions, accounts, and files. A step that depends on an unstated prerequisite fails for the people who need a tutorial most.

### Make every step succeed

Give each step as one action with its expected result, and verify the whole tutorial from a clean start ([make examples runnable](documentation.md#make-examples-runnable)). A failed step teaches the reader that they are the problem.

### Show results as you go

After each step, say what the reader should see, so they can tell they are on track.

### Do not explain or branch

Give the shortest path. Link to [explanation](explanation.md) for reasons and to [reference](reference.md) for options instead of presenting them in the steps.

## Exceptions

A tutorial for experienced readers new to a product may skip basics, but must say whom it assumes.
